Method

Your amount is divided by the price of an ounce in the year you had it, then multiplied by the price of an ounce in the year you are pricing it in. The ounce is the fixed quantity; only the dollars on either side of it move.

Each year is valued at the midpoint of its opening and closing price. That is not the same as the year’s average, and in a violent year the two part company: silver opened 1980 at $39.95 and closed it at $15.50, a midpoint of $27.73 against a true annual mean near $20.98.

From 1968 the prices are the daily London fixes published by the LBMA, taking each year’s first and last fix. Before 1968 there was one published figure per year, so the opening and closing prices are the same: gold at its administered price, $20.67 until 1933 and $35.00 from 1934, and silver at the unit value in USGS Data Series 140, which tracks the annual New York price to within about 0.3%. The CPI-U comparison comes from FRED series CPIAUCNS and begins in 1913.
